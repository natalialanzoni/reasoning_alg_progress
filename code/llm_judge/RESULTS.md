# Behaviour counts — results

**The paper uses `paper_figs/table_behaviours.tex`, built from
`out/judge_whole_gemini.jsonl` (verification) and
`out/judge_backtracking_v6.jsonl` (backtracking).** Rejected methods are in
`data/archive/llm_judge_abandoned/` and feed nothing.

Generated by `code/llm_judge/behaviour_table.py`. Sample: the four
`fig_mechanism` models, 40 competition problems, k=8, CoT only.

**Both behaviours come from an LLM judge** (whole trace, temperature 0):
verification from gemini-2.5-flash, backtracking from gemini-3.1-pro-preview. The two halves use different prompts, settled at different times:
verification **v0** (Gandhi et al.'s template plus a one-line domain edit,
approved on manual review) and backtracking **v6** (see README for why v0–v5
were replaced, and for v6's validation against a hand-counted key). The string markers below are a diagnostic kept here, not a row in
the paper table — they measure a narrower construct.

## The denominator is REASONING tokens

Both behaviours are counted in the CoT, so the per-token rate divides by CoT
tokens, not by the whole completion. This was wrong until 2026-09-24: it divided
by `total_completion_tokens`, which includes the written-up answer, while the
judge only ever saw the CoT.

GLM logs `thinking_tokens`, so its columns are exact (`thinking + answer = total`,
checked). **gpt-oss logs no reasoning-token field at all** — only `n_tokens` for
the whole completion — so its CoT share is estimated by character proportion
(median 0.844). Treat the two gpt-oss columns as approximate to about 1%.

The fix matters for one claim: scale verification per 10k moves from 0.91× to
**0.99×**. Verification density is flat with scale, not falling.

## The two levers

| lever | median reasoning tokens | verif /trace | verif /10k | backtrack /10k | markers /10k |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Scale** gpt-oss 20B→120B | 0.60× | 0.48× | 0.99× | 1.16× | 0.55× |
| **Algorithm** GLM 5.2→5.3 | 0.28× | 0.73× | 1.23× | 1.90× | 4.35× |

Backtracking v3 (gemini-2.5-flash) gave 1.17× and 1.94× on the same traces, so the
v3 → v6 change moves the levers by under 3%. v6's absolute counts are lower for GLM
(5.2: 2.67 per trace against v3's 3.90).

## Reading this

**Per trace, both levers cut behaviour counts mainly by cutting trace length. Per
token, verification is flat to slightly up and backtracking rises.** The
defensible headline is that efficiency gains come mostly from *writing less*, not
from *reasoning differently* — with the caveat that backtracking density does move
on the algorithm lever, by 1.90×.

**Scale (20B → 120B).** Verification falls 0.48× per trace but is unchanged per
token (0.99×); backtracking density rises 1.16×. The bigger model writes about 40%
shorter traces at the same verification density.

**Algorithm (5.2 → 5.3).** Median trace length collapses to 0.28×. Verification count
falls 1.38× while length falls 1.66×, so density *rises* 1.23× — verification is
preserved relative to length rather than increased. Backtracking density rises
1.90×, the largest effect in the table.

**The markers disagree on size, not direction.** They give 4.35× for the algorithm
lever against the judge's 1.90×. They count only explicit abandonment language, so
they are narrower by construction, and they have known recall gaps — the set lacks
"another approach" and "step wrong", both of which appear in traces where the
judge correctly found instances the markers missed.

## All traces, or correct traces only?

The table above pools all judged traces: 1,245 verification (981 correct, 264
incorrect). `behaviour_table.py --correct-only` restricts to traces that reached
the right answer; with `--tex paper_figs/table_behaviours_correct.tex` it writes
the paper's correct-only table.

Restricting raises every rate substantially — incorrect traces are long and
verification-sparse — and moves the algorithm backtracking lever from 1.90× to
2.85×. **The scale backtracking lever nearly vanishes on correct traces: 1.16× →
1.05×.** The rise in backtracking density with scale is carried by the incorrect
traces, so "backtracking rises with scale" should not be stated without that
qualifier. The selection concern is mild, because the pass-rate gap *within* each
lever is small (20b 69% vs 120b 74%; GLM 5.2 85% vs 5.3 87%), so the two columns
of a comparison are not built on very different problem mixes. **Whichever the
paper uses, the prose must say which** — the two differ by up to 44%.

## 2 backtracking traces could not be judged

v6 returned no count for 2 of 1,280 traces, both gpt-oss-20b
(`aime_2026_ii_13` s3 and `hmmt_2026_feb_geo_08` s7). On both the judge ran away:
one listed 35+ entries until it hit the 65,536-token output cap, and the other
wrote ~63k tokens with no entries in the required format. Six further replies were
empty provider errors; the run retries those, and all six succeeded.

The loss is 1.3% of gpt-oss-20b's reasoning tokens and 0% of every other model's.
It touches only the scale lever, and barely:

| lever | observed | imputed at 20b's own long-trace rate | worst case, missing = 0 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Scale 20b→120b | 1.16× | 1.16× | 1.17× |

(v3 lost 25 traces, up to 10.8% of one model's tokens, pointing along both levers.)

## The open question

**Partly resolved.** The bullets below were measured on the **v0** backtracking
prompt, which review rejected. v3 and then v6 replaced it (README has the history),
and `for_RA_review/v6_backtracking/` is the current adjudication. The
observations are kept because the underlying worry — that the instrument, not the
behaviour, drives the effect size — is not retired by changing the prompt.

* The judge's individual calls verified well where checked: on
  `gpt-oss-20b hmmt_2026_feb_geo_09` it found 2 instances and quoted both
  verbatim, where the markers found 1.
* Under v0, across 1,253 traces its count correlated more with how often the
  model writes "wait" (r = +0.50) than with explicit
  abandonment language (r = +0.35), and its justifications sometimes cited
  *"expresses uncertainty ('Hmm.')"* — self-interruption, not abandonment. This is
  what v2 over-corrected for and v3 re-balanced; it has not been re-measured
  under v3.
* Two judges (gemini-2.5-flash, sonnet-4.5) correlated only **+0.52** on
  backtracking, exact agreement 9/40, and support opposite lever directions.
  On verification the same pair preserves the model ranking despite ~2.5×
  magnitude differences — which is why verification is the stable measurement and
  backtracking is not.

The marker cross-check is no longer a row in the paper table (it measures a
narrower construct, and printing it alongside invites a reader to treat two
definitions as one measurement). It stays in this file and in the console output.
**State in the paper that the backtracking effect size is instrument-dependent** —
the same traces give 1.90× on the judge and 4.35× on the markers, and v0/v2 of the
judge prompt gave materially different numbers on the same data (v3 and v6 agree
to within 3% on both levers).

## Rates are pooled

`sum(counts) / sum(tokens) × 10⁴`, not the mean of per-trace ratios. The mean of
ratios does not divide: for a sparse behaviour concentrated in long traces it
implied 19,584 tokens per gpt-oss-20b trace against a true mean of 12,494, so
per-trace ÷ per-10k gave a number matching nothing and a reviewer would catch it.
Pooled rates divide exactly. Switching moved the verification scale lever from
0.70× to 0.91× — that effect was largely an artifact of the statistic. The
CoT-denominator fix then took the same lever to 0.99×, so what once read as a 30%
fall in verification density with scale is, correctly measured, no change at all.

## The memory-recall confound

GLM 5.3 spends much of its trace trying to remember whether it has seen the
problem — *"let me reconsider the memory one final time... I believe the answer is
63"*. That inflates the **marker** count (it fires `let me reconsider` with no path
abandoned), so marker hits within 400 characters of recall language are dropped:
578/1,346 removed for GLM 5.3, and 2/277, 1/208, 0/55 for the others.

Reproduce with `python code/llm_judge/recall_check.py`. Everything below is
restricted to claims made **before the model computes anything** — the only
position where a number cannot have been derived.

| model | traces attempting recall | traces stating a remembered number | claims correct |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| gpt-oss-20b | 1/320 (0%) | 0/320 (0%) | — |
| gpt-oss-120b | 2/320 (1%) | 0/320 (0%) | — |
| GLM 5.2 | 1/320 (0%) | 0/320 (0%) | — |
| **GLM 5.3** | **95/320 (30%)** | **56/320 (18%)** | **0/83 (0%)** |

**Zero of 83 pre-computation claims match gold.** With n=83 that bounds a true
recall rate at roughly 1 in 83 rather than proving it is exactly zero.

**Read the two columns separately, because they support different claims.**

*The behavioural finding* is about GLM 5.3 alone: it opens 30% of its traces
reaching for memory, commits to a specific remembered number in 18%, and then
derives the answer anyway. The other three models essentially never do this — four
traces between them, none stating a number. This is a qualitative split, not a
difference of degree.

*The contamination finding* rests entirely on GLM 5.3 too, because all 83 scorable
claims come from it. For the other three, "never attempts retrieval" is consistent
with no contamination but is not a test of it — a model cannot fail a test it does
not take.

The naive test — scoring every recall claim regardless of position — returns
16–40% and ranks GLM 5.2 highest despite GLM 5.2 making no pre-computation
attempts at all. Those are post-hoc restatements of an already-derived answer, not
memory. That column is not reported anywhere here because it cannot be read
correctly.

So this is **confabulated retrieval, not contamination**. GLM 5.3 attempts recall
~60–100× more than any other model and fails completely, citing 2024 most (1,054
times), then 2025, 2020, 2014 — never 2026, because its training predates the
benchmark.

Caveat: "before computation" is a heuristic — the first `=` with a numeric
right-hand side, `\boxed`, `\frac`, or an explicit compute/calculate. A model
reasoning quantitatively in prose first would slip past it.

## What these numbers are not

* **Absolute counts are instrument-dependent.** Two judges differ ~2.5× in
  magnitude on verification even where they agree on ranking. Compare models
  within this table; do not quote "GLM 5.2 verifies 13 times".
* **Backtracking is unresolved** — see the open question above.
* **5.2 → 5.3 confounds everything that changed between releases** — RL recipe,
  data, output format. "Denser behaviour in shorter traces" is the measurement;
  attributing it to RL is an interpretation.
* **Verification's 1.21× is weak.** With n≈305 per model and wide per-trace
  spread, treat it as "unchanged" unless a confidence interval says otherwise.
